# Settlement Generation

Settlements are inhabited outposts scattered across the Saltglass Steppe, where pilgrims, traders, and faction members gather around sources of water and shelter. Each settlement reflects the influence of the factions that control it, manifesting their architectural preferences and social hierarchies in glass and salt-weathered stone.

## Settlement Tiers

**Villages** (80x60) emerge around natural springs or storm-glass deposits. Small clusters of weathered structures house 20 souls who eke out survival through scavenging and minor trade. Buildings cluster loosely, connected by worn paths through the salt.

**Towns** (120x90) form at crossroads where trade routes converge. With populations of 50, they feature more substantial structures—trading posts, workshops, and faction outposts. The dominant faction's architectural style shapes the settlement's character.

**Cities** (180x120) are rare bastions of civilization, housing 100 inhabitants behind walls of fused glass. Multiple factions compete for influence, creating districts with distinct architectural themes. These settlements anchor entire regions of the Steppe.

## Faction Influence

The controlling faction determines which structures appear in a settlement. Mirror Monk settlements feature meditation chambers and glass workshops. Salt Trader towns center around markets and warehouses. Storm Cult villages cluster around weather-watching towers and ritual circles.

When multiple factions hold significant influence (>25% control), their architectural styles blend, creating settlements where glass spires rise beside salt-brick warehouses, and Archive data terminals hum next to traditional forges.

## Inhabitants

Each settlement spawns NPCs based on the structures present. Merchants staff trading posts, guards patrol faction outposts, and innkeepers tend to waystation lodges. These inhabitants carry faction loyalties, local knowledge, and goods appropriate to their settlement's tier and controlling factions.

The storm-touched landscape ensures no two settlements are identical—even with the same faction control, the Steppe's harsh conditions and available materials create unique variations in every outpost.
